# People, positions and hiring: design

Status: phases 1–4 are built (sections 3.5, 4.3, 5.1 and 6.6). Free mock/offline
checks pass; real simulation acceptance runs are left to the user. Four
phases, in order:

1. **People and traits**: a people server that generates characters (temperament, faculties,
   circumstances, a polished persona), tried first on the existing casts.
2. **Positions**: separate who someone is from the position they hold, with a shared catalogue
   of positions, a situation for each character and a higher agent cap.
3. **A small test world**: a cottage-sized world on Claudeworth's mechanics, so traits and hiring
   can be tried in real runs of a minute or two.
4. **Hiring**: find a candidate, meet them, engage them or send them away, with an away status
   for people who leave.

## 1. Why

In `runs/claudeworth-20261018-111357` the earl told Hale to "engage a proper man from the
village" to drive the Daimler, with a character from the butcher and a wage from the shopkeeper.
Hale spent most of the day on it:

- Hobbs named "Tom Ridding, at the smithy's yard" and "Jem Tyler" in conversation. Neither
  exists anywhere in the world.
- Hobbs's `spend-time` made a real item, a "Sealed character for Tom Ridding", vouching for a man
  who doesn't exist.
- Briggs advised a wage ("£1 10s a week, livery found").
- Then nothing could happen. There's no way to meet Tom Ridding, engage him, or have him drive.

Two smaller findings from the same run:

- The reference never changed hands. Hobbs said "Here it is" and Hale "I have it safe" in a
  conversation, but speaking turns can't `give`, and `map.edn` shows Hobbs still holds it (see
  roadmap C14).
- Nobody can drive the Daimler, and travel is individual (the earl walked to the village alone).
  A chauffeur needs vehicle and passenger mechanics before the job means anything. That's out of
  scope here; phase 2's catalogue just lists the position.

## 2. The user's decisions (2026-10-18)

1. Before the meeting, a candidate is only a record. They're spawned as an agent when they come
   to meet, at a set meeting place (Claudeworth: the carriage sweep).
2. Candidates are kept in a pool of limited size (first in, first out), in case they're mentioned
   again later.
3. Positions aren't permanently attached to a person, and needn't be tightly restricted or
   checked.
4. Traits are Big Five descriptors plus period traits. Traits shape the agent but are hidden from
   the interviewer. A reputation from the recommender or another source is separate from the
   traits. Existing characters need traits too, so traits and positions are a separate job, done
   first.
5. Anyone may hire. The agents decide whether that's proper.
6. Candidates may refuse or haggle, and may make up whatever conditions they like. The
   conditions aren't enforced for now.
7. The agent cap is too low and should be much higher.
8. There should be a bigger list of positions to fill. Positions are tangled up with character
   details now; that is untangled together with traits, before hiring.
9. People who aren't hired, or are dismissed, get an away status.
10. Candidates can be hired the same day.
11. Each Big Five axis has three levels.
12. Agents should know a hire needs a free bed, but code doesn't enforce it; they decide for
    themselves.
13. New people are generated by an MCP server.
14. Each person holds one position. Holding several may come later, but only with a way to take
    up and give up official positions so people can move between them. For now a person keeps
    the position they have.
15. `send-for` is right for now. Later it should fit the simulation better, for example by having
    to send for a candidate through whoever gave the reference. The worry is that agents won't
    know how to send for someone, so the engine must tell them plainly.
16. One candidate per recommendation.
17. The server generates base traits, and a model then polishes them into the character's data.
18. Characters know all their own traits, for now.
19. The server rolls and generates the existing characters too, as the first test of it.
20. A smaller test world comes before hiring, because real runs of Claudeworth take too long to
    test these things.
21. The Big Five is augmented with the Victorian phrenological faculties: one faculty from each
    group is rolled at random, low, average or high.
22. Circumstances stay, alongside temperament and faculties.
23. The polish call is made by the engine, not the server.
24. The existing characters are rolled with no pins; fixes come later if needed.
25. Generated people use the same default model as everyone else (`deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash`).
26. The pool holds 40 people.
27. Each character gets a `:situation` field for what is neither their job nor their personality
    (the earl means to enlarge the house; the cook has just joined). How a situation changes
    during a run is a later question.
28. The butcher and the shopkeeper become separate positions.
29. The catalogue of positions is shared between worlds, so small test worlds can reuse it.
30. The clerk path and the optional characters (`:architect?`, `:housemaid?`) are left alone for
    now, to be dealt with separately; roadmap C13 stays open.
31. Positions carry forms of address, behind a flag that is easy to turn off, so runs with and
    without explicit forms of address can be compared.
32. No real runs during development; the user runs them afterwards.
33. Each person has a public **appearance**: what anyone sees at a glance (age, build, dress,
    bearing), written by the polish with the persona and shown to others in the view of the people
    present and in `look`. It never states or names the hidden traits. Done before phase 3
    ("Appearance as built", section 4.3).

## 3. Phase 1: people and traits

**Current generation contract: section 3.7 supersedes the descriptor/circumstance-template design below.**

### 3.1 A person's traits

A person has three kinds of traits, all hidden from everyone but themselves (decisions 4 and 18).

**Temperament: the Big Five,** three levels each (decision 11):

| Axis | Measures | High reads as | Low reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| openness | curiosity, imagination, willingness to entertain new ideas | quick, imaginative, full of ideas | practical, conventional, distrusts new notions |
| conscientiousness | self-control, diligence, attention to detail | always prepared, orderly, keeps to a schedule | leaves things about, shirks, slapdash |
| extraversion | boldness, energy, sociability | talkative, starts conversations, likes company | quiet, keeps in the background, reserved with strangers |
| agreeableness | kindness, helpfulness, willingness to cooperate | soft-hearted, obliging, puts people at ease | self-interested, suspicious, quarrelsome |
| neuroticism | irritability, anxiety, low spirits | worries, easily upset, moody, quick-tempered | calm, even-tempered, seldom low |

Each level of each axis has several period-worded descriptors in the server's data, so two people
at the same level don't read alike. The IPIP sample items ("I am always prepared", "I leave my
belongings around") are a good base, rewritten in period English. The IPIP items are reported to
be public domain; check before copying any wording.

**Faculties: the phrenological organs** (decision 21). Phrenology was still the period's popular
vocabulary of character, which is the point: it's how a 1904 reference or a gossiping neighbour
might describe someone ("a fine head for Order, but small Cautiousness"). Treat it as period
flavour, not psychology. One faculty is rolled from each of the seven groups, at the
phrenologists' own sizes, **small, moderate or large**:

| Group | Faculties |
|---|---|
| Propensities | Amativeness (sexual attraction), Philoprogenitiveness (love of offspring), Concentrativeness (keeping the mind on one thing; attachment to place), Adhesiveness (friendship, attachment), Combativeness (courage, readiness to oppose), Destructiveness (severity, energy to harm), Alimentiveness (appetite), Secretiveness (tact, concealment), Acquisitiveness (desire to possess), Constructiveness (urge to build and make) |
| Lower sentiments | Self-Esteem (pride), Love of Approbation (vanity, ambition), Cautiousness (fear, prudence) |
| Superior sentiments | Benevolence, Veneration (reverence, respect for authority), Firmness (perseverance), Conscientiousness (justice and duty), Hope, Wonder (love of the novel and marvellous), Ideality (love of beauty, the poetic), Wit (sense of the ludicrous), Imitation (mimicry) |
| External senses | Feeling (touch), Taste, Smell, Hearing, Sight |
| Knowing faculties: objects | Individuality (observation), Form (memory for shapes and faces), Size, Weight (balance), Colouring |
| Knowing faculties: relations | Locality (sense of direction), Number (arithmetic), Order (love of arrangement), Eventuality (memory for events), Time, Tune (musical ability), Language (words) |
| Reflecting faculties | Comparison (analogy), Causality (cause and effect) |

So everyone gets seven readings, such as "large Acquisitiveness, small Cautiousness, large
Veneration, small Hearing, large Form, moderate Number, small Causality." A small external sense
is a physical fact (short-sighted, hard of hearing) that the polish should make concrete.
Conscientiousness appears in both schemes; when both are rolled, the polish reconciles them.

**Circumstances**: one or two period facts, as distinct from dispositions. They're drawn from
groups so contradictory pairs (drinks and teetotal) aren't rolled together, and a position can
weight them (a gamekeeper is more likely ex-army):

- **Habits:** drinks, teetotal (signed the pledge), smokes a pipe, a gambler (horses, cards).
- **Faith and opinion:** chapel, Church and King, a free-thinker, a Radical who reads the papers,
  superstitious.
- **Ties and secrets:** a sweetheart in the village, a child put out to nurse, owes money, sends
  wages home to a widowed mother, left the last place under a cloud.
- **Background:** ex-army (South Africa), came up through the workhouse, a farm labourer's son,
  in service since twelve, reads and writes well, cannot read.

### 3.2 The people server

`mcp-servers/people/`, a stateless babashka MCP server like the architect's (decision 13). The
engine passes everything it needs, including a seed key, so the same request gets the same
person, and the result is recorded as an event, so replay never calls out.

- **Data files**, as the architect has `prices.edn`: `temperament.edn` (Big Five descriptors per
  axis and level), `faculties.edn` (the seven groups, with descriptors per size),
  `circumstances.edn` (groups and position weights), `names.edn` (first names and surnames for
  rural England around 1900, by sex). Other settings would add their own files.
- **`generate_people {position count area setting seed taken-names}`** returns base records:
  name (not in `taken-names`), sex, an age suited to the position, origin, where they live, the
  five temperament levels, seven faculty readings and one or two circumstances, each with its
  descriptor text. It's deterministic and free.
- **`reputation {person knower relation seed}`** returns what one person knows of another: how
  well they know them (by sight, a neighbour, ten years' acquaintance) and which of the hidden
  traits they know, rolled from the relation.

### 3.3 The polish

A base record reads like a phrenologist's chart, so a model writes the person (decision 17). The
engine makes one narrow call, as it does for the shop's appraiser: it uses the manor key, the
call is recorded in `llm.edn` with its cost, and it goes to a cheap model. The call gets the
setting, the position and the base record, plus any authored notes. It returns checked JSON:

- `persona`: three or four sentences in the voice of the existing personas, which make the
  traits concrete. "Small Hearing" becomes "a little deaf in the left ear"; "large Acquisitiveness"
  becomes "counts every penny and keeps a tin of savings under the boards." At most 1200 characters.
- `manner`: one sentence on how they come across to a stranger, for their own guidance in
  presenting themselves (a candidate will present well, as real applicants do).

Code checks the lengths and that the name and facts are unchanged before the result is stored.
The agent's prompt ("Who you are") gets the persona, then a short line of the readings, so the
agent knows all its traits (decision 18). The raw record stays in state for `reputation` and
later references.

The engine makes the call, not the server (decision 23), as it does for the shop's appraiser and
the work judge. The architect server makes no model calls and has no key, and the people server
stays the same: deterministic, free and keyless.

### 3.4 The existing casts as the first test

Decision 19: a dev command rolls and polishes every character in a world file, for example
`(roll-cast! "worlds/claudeworth.edn")` at the REPL. It writes the base record and polished
persona into the world file for review in `git diff`. Loading a world never calls out; the world
file holds the result.

Authored characters have fixed points (the earl is lazy and a womaniser; the countess is easily
angered). For now everything is rolled (decision 24), so the earl may come out small in
Amativeness; the polish keeps each character's name, position and situation from the old persona,
not its traits. Pins (`:pin {:amativeness :large}`) can come later if a rolled cast needs fixing.

Phase 1 is done when the server and its data exist with tests (free, seeded, no model);
the polish is a checked call with a mock responder; `roll-cast!` has rolled Claudeworth's cast
and you've reviewed the result; and prompts show each agent its own traits and no one else's.

### 3.5 Phase 1 as built

Done. The contract is in "People and traits" in `docs/scenario-authoring.md`.

- **The server** is `mcp-servers/people/` (babashka, stateless, no model, no key), with the four
  data files, all in wording written for it, plus a fifth, `positions.edn` (below).
  `generate_people` and `reputation` are deterministic from their seed. Tests in
  `test/manor/people_test.clj` check determinism, five axes, one faculty per group, one or two
  circumstances from different groups never in an excluded pair, untaken names and the position
  weights, and they check `reputation` against the person's own traits.
- **The polish** is `manor.mechanics.people/polish!`: one call with one correction, a free `mock-polish`,
  and checks that the persona names the person, keeps to the lengths and uses no jargon.
- **Traits on agents:** world-file agents may carry `:sex :manner :temperament :faculties
  :circumstances` and a `:sketch`. The agent's own prompt shows the persona, then the manner and
  a line of readings. A test proves no other agent's view or prompt contains them, and that an
  agent without traits gets the same prompt as before.
- **`roll-cast!`** (`dev/user.clj`, over `manor.mechanics.people/roll-cast!`) writes into the world file
  in place, keeping comments and layout. Loading a world never calls out.
- **Claudeworth's cast** was rolled with seed 1 by `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash`. The first roll
  cost $0.0046 for 13 calls. Seven characters were written; Lord Claudeworth and Nellie overshot
  the 600 characters on both tries. After the fix below, a second roll of the earl, Nellie and
  Hobbs cost $0.0015, with every answer accepted first time. Hobbs was included because his
  persona had lost "meat is his trade, not general groceries", and the second roll lost it too
  (see section 8). The records are in `old-test-runs/roll-cast-claudeworth-first` and
  `old-test-runs/roll-cast-claudeworth-earl-nellie-hobbs` in the phase 1 worktree.

Deviations and decisions made while building:

1. **`positions.edn`**, a fifth data file: the age range for a position, and its sex where the
   place was a man's or a woman's in 1904 (a housemaid, a groom). Without it, "an age suited to
   the position" had no data.
2. **`generate_people` takes an optional `sex`,** which overrides the position's. `roll-cast!`
   passes the agent's `:sex`, so authored characters keep theirs. `:sex` was added by hand to
   Claudeworth's nine agents before the roll, taken from their honorifics and pronouns.
3. **`origins` and `dwellings`** live in `names.edn`. `setting` is accepted only as
   `"england-1900"`, the one setting that has data.
4. **Authored characters drop the server's age, origin and home.** The polish takes them from
   the old persona instead, so Daisy stays sixteen.
5. **`:sketch`:** `roll-cast!` keeps the old persona as `:sketch` and polishes from it on later
   rolls, so a re-roll starts from the authored text and not from a rolled persona. The sketch
   reaches no prompt.
6. **`:manner`** goes in the agent's own prompt after the persona ("To a stranger you come across
   as: ..."), because it guides how they present themselves.
7. **The persona length is advised, not just limited.** The model is asked for about 900
   characters and code accepts up to 1200 (the same pattern as aspect values), because deepseek
   overshot a plain "at most 1200" for three of nine characters. `{:only [names]}` re-rolls just
   the agents named.
8. **"Facts unchanged"** is enforced by storing the facts from the record, never from the model.
   The text check covers only what text can show: the person is named (any word of the name but
   a title), and there's no jargon (faculty or Big Five axis names, phrenology).
9. **Temperament levels** are `:low :average :high`, rolled evenly. Faculty sizes are `:small
   :moderate :large`, as in section 3.1.
10. **Labels:** "a farm labourer's son" is written "a farm labourer's child", so that it suits
    anyone. Descriptors are written so that they suit a man or a woman.

### 3.6 Fresh personalities on re-roll (supersedes the sketch preservation above)

The user wants completely new personalities, not rewrites of the original descriptions.
`roll-cast!` now sends the polish only the setting, position and freshly generated record,
with the existing character's name. It never passes the old sketch, persona, manner,
appearance, traits, or situation. Generated age and origin are retained; generated home is
omitted, since homes are already part of the world setup. Name, sex, position, situation,
bedroom, routine and other authored setup stay unchanged.

*Added 2026-10-23:* the agent's `:situation` now reaches the polish as its `:notes`, which
`polish-request` and the instructions always supported ("when notes are supplied, preserve their
authored identity, situation and directions"). Without it a rolled cast contradicted its scenario:
rolling `claudeworth-medium`'s household made the seventeen-year-old daughter twelve with the
housekeeping on her hands, and gave the baroness grown-up children. Only the situation is passed;
the old persona, manner, appearance, traits and circumstances still never are.

Each invocation uses a fresh seed unless an explicit `:seed` is supplied; the seed is returned,
printed by the REPL helper and recorded in `roll-cast.edn` for reproduction. Persona, manner,
appearance, temperament, faculties and circumstances are overwritten on success. A successful
roll removes any legacy sketch; failed characters and unselected characters are untouched.
The obsolete sketches have been removed from Claudeworth and Lodge. Regression tests inspect
the actual polish input to prove there is no sketch/persona fallback or other old-character
input, and exercise seed recording, repeatability, selective rolls and failure preservation.
Implementation validation used mock writers only.

### 3.7 Basic traits, model-written circumstances (current)

The user rejected the fixed behavioural descriptor and circumstance catalogues. Implemented:

- The server rolls five temperament levels and one faculty/size per group, with unchanged
  selection distributions. It returns readings and short definitions only. All behavioural
  templates are removed; `circumstances.edn` is deleted. Removing descriptor draws changes
  exact old-seed outputs, not the sampling distributions.
- The existing character-writing call returns persona, manner, appearance **and one or two
  written circumstances**. Circumstances are external background, commitments, relationships
  or difficulties, not dispositions or enduring habits. The writer takes inspiration from the
  whole record, including role, without making traits a mechanical destiny. Ordinary lives
  are allowed. It may invent off-stage relatives or past employment, but not connections to
  existing named characters or new present jobs, authority, capabilities, money, items,
  property or actionable assets. Background prose changes no engine state.
- Basic values remain the server's values. The private prompt retains readings and definitions
  alongside prose, under **Faculties**, without a theoretical qualifier. The writer treats these
  as actual characteristics. Clearer period wording is deferred; it must retain the basic
  data, never replace it with a stock story or habit.
- Input projection strips legacy descriptors, circumstances and prose, including mock context.
  Validation checks structure/counts/lengths/blanks only, never judges prose. Circumstance groups,
  position weights and exclusions no longer exist.
- Written circumstances join the completed record before reputation selection/storage.
  Reputation returns sampled readings and existing written circumstances; the speaking agent
  interprets these itself, without another model call or behavioural templates.
- Runtime appearance stays separately written from visible fields only. Candidates store the
  completed record privately; arrival copies written circumstances to the agent. Events own
  generated results; replay/load/branch never regenerate them. Old map-style circumstances and
  descriptors in saved events remain readable; no saved run is rewritten.
- Tests cover basic-only input, source shape, circumstance validation, privacy, reputation,
  arrival/replay, cast replacement/failure and budgets. The mock writer is a test fixture only.
  Cast writing now supports `:max-cost` across calls/corrections; the REPL default is $0.20.

## 4. Phase 2: positions

### 4.1 What's tangled now

- A role's `:about` in `claudeworth.edn` mixes the position with the individual: "You are Mr
  Hale, private secretary…", "You are Nellie, the housemaid… You are somewhat naive and foolish",
  and the earl's laziness is in both `:about` and `:persona`. A second housemaid would be told
  she is Nellie.
- An agent's `:role` is one keyword, fixed at spawn, and it does three jobs: which brief they
  read, which tools they get (`actions/allowed?`: `quote-goods` for `#{:shopkeeper}`), and what
  code treats them as (`building` pays commission fees to an `:architect`, `shop` accepts a
  `:shopkeeper` as clerk).
- The scenario's code map `claudeworth/roles` lists every role keyword. A spawned agent with a
  new role would have no brief.
- Bedrooms and routines are read from the world file's agents at `init` into
  `[:state :bedrooms]` and `[:state :routines]`. Nothing updates them for someone who arrives
  later.
- `sleep` already works in any bedroom and doesn't check whose it is. Each agent's view shows
  only their own bedroom, so nobody can see which beds are free.
- `:limits :max-agents` defaults to 10 and Claudeworth sets none, so the GM's `spawn-agent` would
  stop at the 10th participant. Claudeworth already has 9. The cap only exists to stop a GM
  spawning without bound (the operator's `spawn!` ignores it, roadmap C2).

### 4.2 The model

| Thing | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Person** | name, sex, age, origin, traits, persona | the agent (and, for candidates, the pool record) |
| **Position** | a job in the world: title, duties, standing, workplace, address, capabilities | a shared catalogue, which a world file can add to |
| **Situation** | what is neither job nor personality: plans, newness, who to see | the agent, in the world file |
| **Post** | that this person holds this position, for whom, since when, on what terms | state, changed by events |
| **Reputation** | what someone knows of a person | a record per knower, given privately |

**Positions.** The catalogue is a shared data file (decision 29), for example
`resources/positions/england-1904.edn`. A world file names the catalogue it uses and may add or
override entries in its own `:roles`. The people server's age and sex ranges move into the same
entries (the engine passes them in the request), so there is one list of positions, not two.
Each position has:

- `:title` ("Housemaid"), and `:about`: generic duties with no names or personality ("Your day is
  housework: dusting and sweeping…")
- `:standing`: `:family`, `:upper-servant`, `:servant`, `:outdoor`, `:tradesman` or `:villager`.
  This informs forms of address and who sleeps where.
- `:workplace` (optional location ids) and `:sleeps` (`:in-house` or `:out`)
- `:capabilities` (optional): what code treats the holder as (`:shop-clerk`, `:architect`).
- `:addressed-as` (optional): how the holder is addressed ("my lord", "Mr" plus surname, a
  Christian name). With the address flag on (decision 31), a person's own prompt says how they
  are addressed and everyone's view of the people around them shows how to address each one.
  With it off, no form of address appears anywhere, including the `:about` texts, and the agents
  decide for themselves. It's a run option (`:addressed-as?`, default on) with a CLI flag.

One position is `:none` (no post; a villager between places), so candidates and the dismissed
have one.

A starting catalogue for a country house and village in 1904:

- Family and upstairs: earl, countess, son or daughter of the house, private secretary,
  governess, tutor, nurse.
- Upper servants: butler, housekeeper, cook, valet, lady's maid.
- Servants: footman, hall boy, head housemaid, housemaid, between maid, kitchen maid, scullery
  maid, laundry maid, odd man, maid-of-all-work.
- Outdoor: chauffeur, coachman, groom, stable lad, head gardener, under-gardener, gamekeeper,
  estate carpenter, lodge-keeper.
- Village: shopkeeper, butcher, architect, blacksmith, publican, postmistress, carrier, vicar,
  doctor, schoolmistress, labourer.

Only positions with mechanics have capabilities. Everyone else works through `spend-time` and
the work judge, as Nellie and Daisy do now.

**Posts.** `[:state :posts]` maps a person id to `{:position :housemaid :household "household"
:since round :terms nil}`. One position each (decision 14). The agent's `:role` stays as the
brief and tool key, kept equal to the position id, so the engine's role machinery keeps working.
One event, `:people/post-changed`, updates both. Positions aren't checked against who holds them
(decision 3): two cooks, or none, are allowed. Nothing here should rule out several positions per
person later, with take-up and give-up actions.

**Tools by capability.** Actions that need a capability say so, as `:roles #{:shopkeeper}` does
now. Either `:roles` takes capability keywords and `actions/allowed?` looks them up through the
position, or the agent's capabilities are a separate set and actions gain a `:needs` key. The
code that checks `(= :architect (:role ...))` checks the capability instead.

**Situations** (decision 27). Each agent may have `:situation` in the world file, shown in their
own prompt as "Your situation", after "Who you are". The polish never rewrites it. Claudeworth's
situations come out of yesterday's stopgap role texts: the earl's plan to enlarge the house and
Pugh as the man to see, the countess's wish to be consulted about it, the cook's being new to the
house, Hale's errands for the earl.

**The butcher** (decision 28) gets its own position, `:butcher`, beside `:shopkeeper`; both have
the `:shop-clerk` capability, and each states its own trade in its duties.

**Briefs.** The scenario's code map of roles goes away: every non-GM agent gets `agent-brief`,
which reads the position's `:about` from state. A position added to the world file needs no
code.

**Bedrooms and routines** move into state keyed by person, set by events
(`:people/bedroom-assigned`, `:people/routine-set`) so a hire can have them too. Bedrooms stay

### 6.1 Candidates and the pool

A candidate is a record in `[:state :pool]`: the people server's base record and polished
persona, plus:

```clojure
{:seeks [:chauffeur :groom]                     ; positions they'd take
 :candidate-home "a cottage by the pond, in the next parish" ; off-map background, not a map id
 :known-to #{"hale" "hobbs"}                    ; who has heard of them
 :status :pooled}                               ; :pooled, :sent-for, :here, :engaged, :away
```

The pool holds at most 40 records (decision 26), first in, first out. Records that are `:sent-for`,
`:here` or `:engaged`, or that belong to an agent in play, are never evicted. An agent who has
heard of someone keeps their notes after the record goes, but the person can no longer be sent
for.

### 6.2 Finding people: `recommend`

`recommend {position for}` is a query that anyone can make on their turn, for someone present,
as Briggs makes `quote-goods` for a customer. A butcher knows people; so might Nellie's mother.

1. The engine looks in the pool for someone who seeks that position and whom the recommender
   could plausibly know (same area). If there's nobody, it asks the people server for one person
   and polishes them. One candidate per recommendation (decision 16).
2. The engine asks the server what the recommender knows of the candidate, and records it as
   `:people/reputation-known`, visible only to the recommender. The candidate's name and the
   positions they seek are shared with the asker too (`:known-to`).
3. The reply tells the recommender who the candidate is and what they know of them. The
   recommender then speaks in their own words, so what they say can be honest, kind, slanted or
   a lie, and is grounded either way: Hobbs says "Tom Ridding" because the record exists, and
   whatever he says of him is his choice, made knowing the truth.
4. The asker's event line says plainly how to meet the candidate: "To meet Tom Ridding, use
   `send-for` with his id" (decision 15).

A written character is then an ordinary item made by work, linked to the candidate's id, and the
work judge can be shown what the writer knows.

### 6.3 Meeting: `send-for`

`send-for {candidate at?}` from anyone who knows of the candidate:

- Same day (decision 10): the candidate arrives at the meeting place (the scenario's
  `:meeting-place`) at the start of the next round, or at a stated time.
- Arrival spawns the agent from the record: name, persona and traits, position `:none`, the
  run's default model (decision 25), and a brief saying they've come about the place and who
  sent for them. They're told nothing they couldn't know.
- They're an ordinary agent from then on: `converse`, `walk` if invited in, `look`. The interview
  is just conversation; there's no interview tool.
- Later (decision 15): sending for someone goes through whoever recommended them. The asker
  asks the recommender to send word, and only the recommender, or someone who knows where the
  candidate lives, can.

### 6.4 Engaging, haggling and sending away

- `engage {who position terms?}` (final, anyone, decision 5) makes an offer to a candidate who is
  present. `terms` is free text (wage, duties, time off). A new offer replaces the previous one.
- The candidate answers with `accept-place {conditions?}` or `decline-place {reason?}`.
  Haggling is conversation followed by a new offer (decision 6). Conditions are free text, made
  up by the candidate, recorded on the post with the terms, and not enforced. Both parties can
  see them later in their view.
- Acceptance emits `:people/post-changed` (position, household, terms, conditions), assigns the
  bedroom given in the offer if there is one (unchecked, decision 12), and marks the record
  `:engaged`.
- `send-away {who}`, or the candidate's own `take-leave`, emits `:people/departed`. The record goes
  back to the pool marked as seen at the house. A candidate still unengaged leaves only after
  24 in-world hours since arrival or their last conversation, whichever is later (24 rounds
  without a clock). This replaces the earlier three-round rule: Alice Tidy was removed after
  90 minutes in `claudeworth-medium-20261024-153313` while the household was still fetching her
  for an interview. Until then they remain hireable at their actual map location; briefs tell
  them to wait locally, agree where to be found, and not use `take-leave` merely to await word.

### 6.5 The away status

An engine-level status alongside `:active` and `:retired`:

- `:people/departed {id reason}`: status `:away`, no location, no turns, no model calls, removed
  from views. The narration says they leave and which way they go. Their notes and memory are
  kept.
- `:people/returned {id at}`: status `:active` again, at a location, with their notes.
- Retired stays permanent and away is revivable. Dismissal (later) is `:people/post-changed` to
  `:none` plus departed. A dismissed servant can come back as a candidate elsewhere, or as gossip.
- Unlike hibernation, which keeps a routine character in the world (off-stage but placed), an
  away person is out of the world.

### 6.6 Phase 4 as built

Implemented in `manor.mechanics.hiring`, shared by Claudeworth and Lodge. Only mock/offline
validation was run; the user will do real runs. No generated cast files were re-polished.

- **Recommendations:** `recommend {position for}` costs one query slot. The caller and
  recipient must be distinct; self-recommendation returns an actionable rejection, and
  recommendation audiences are duplicate-safe even if the validation is bypassed. It reuses an
  available candidate in the same walking area, or asks the people MCP server for one,
  writes their appearance, polishes their private persona and asks for a reputation.
  The two character-writer calls use the run's default model, not `:judge-model`.
  The engine records every bridge/model call and cost. Failures publish no partial person;
  the run's `:max-cost` is checked between component calls.
- **Privacy:** appearance has its own short call receiving only age, sex, position and
  names of small external senses. No hidden descriptors, biography, persona, notes, manner
  or full world reach it. The private polish sees this appearance for consistency; its own
  returned appearance is discarded. Neither call adds prose-pattern checks. Full records,
  intermediate bridge results and private spawn data are operator-only events. Only the
  recommender receives their sampled reputation; recommender and asker receive the name,
  candidate id, sought position and explicit `send-for` directions.
- **Pool:** `[:state :pool]` holds at most 40 records in creation order. Eviction removes
  the oldest pooled/away record not belonging to an active agent. Sent-for, present and
  engaged people are protected; a fully protected pool refuses a new recommendation.
  A repeated recommendation reuses the stored reputation for that knower. Proximity uses
  the connected walking area, so Lodge's house/shop domain labels do not divide neighbours.
- **Visits:** `send-for {candidate}` is final and requires a current recommendation. Arrival
  is next round at `:meeting-place` (`front-gate` at Lodge, `carriage-sweep` at Claudeworth),
  before turns, through `spawn-events` and the scenario's `:on-spawn`. Pending arrivals wait
  if the active-agent cap is full. A newcomer holds `:none`, inherits the run's model and
  knows who summoned them. No catalogue position becomes GM-spawnable. Timed appointments
  beyond next-round arrival are not implemented.
- **Offers:** `engage {who position terms? bedroom?}` is final; any character may offer a
  present candidate a non-GM position. Normal turns refresh tools from the live world between
  model steps, so walking to the interview and conversing no longer hides `engage` until the
  next round. This also exposes `send-for` immediately after a recommendation, and removes
  acceptance tools after a free decline, without resetting turn budgets. Speaking turns keep
  their restricted tools; the offer is made after `converse` returns. It replaces an earlier
  offer. `decline-place {reason?}`
  is free; haggling is ordinary conversation. `accept-place {conditions?}` is final, sets the
  role and post together, records employer/terms/conditions, optionally assigns the named
  bedroom and marks the candidate engaged. Both parties retain the agreement in their
  view. Bedroom existence/kind is checked, not availability or sharing; no bed is required
  by code. Acceptance replaces the temporary applicant situation with the new appointment.
- **Leaving:** `send-away {who}` and `take-leave` are final, for unengaged candidates.
  Otherwise a candidate leaves after a full 24 in-world hours since arrival or their last
  conversation, rounded up to whole rounds (24 rounds without a clock). All conversation
  participants refresh that timestamp, not just the initiator. Crossing midnight is not a cutoff.
  Explicit departure remains possible before then; nearby walking/waiting does not end candidacy.
  `:people/departed` makes an agent `:away`, clears location/travel/occupation and stops turns;
  offers close, notes and history remain. Another `send-for` returns the same agent/id with
  those memories through `:people/returned`. Retired people cannot return. Hired staff are
  not subject to the visitor timeout; dismissal and outdoor lodging remain deferred.
- **Candidate homes:** the people server's opt-in `candidate: true` generates a dedicated
  `:candidate-home`, instead of `:lives`, from dwelling and off-map neighbourhood data. It
  never appends the internal walking-area id. `:origin` is not repurposed. The home is kept
  in the base/pool record, reaches the private writer and applicant's brief, and is shown
  only to people who know the candidate. Departure uses it with explicit off-map narration;
  legacy candidates without it leave for "home" rather than an imaginary mapped address.
  This field is exclusively for potential hires; other visitors/couriers and authored cast
  homes are unchanged. A future extension can opt other visitors into home generation without
  changing `:origin`, but no map locations or home-visiting mechanics are added now.
- **Written characters:** `spend-time` may name a known `candidate`. The tool description
  explicitly says to omit it for other activities, and an empty string is treated as absent
  (some models fill optional strings with `""`). Nonempty unknown ids are still rejected.
  The job snapshots only the writer's knowledge for the scene judge. Items made by that job carry `:candidate-id`;
  they are ordinary items and must actually be handed over with `give`, not conversational
  promises. This is not a reference-verification or automatic knowledge-sharing mechanic.
- **Validation:** Lodge's mock day now recommends at 08:30, sends for the candidate at 09:00,
  interviews/offers/accepts at 09:30, and assigns the maid's room. The full 48-round day still
  exercises shopping, work and sleep, with exact replay and no rejected calls or forfeits.
  Dedicated tests cover visible-only appearance input, private events/views, reuse and FIFO,
  refusal/replacement/conditions, departure/return/memory, conversation grace, retired exclusion,
  cap-blocked arrivals, failed bridge/polish rollback, cost-limit interruption and written
  reference context. The user's real-run and transcript review remain pending.
