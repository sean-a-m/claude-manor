Shop orders for delivery are made up and carried by the shop's courier outside this judgement, and arrive by themselves when due: making up, packing or carrying an order makes nothing, so never make the ordered goods or a parcel of them. The scene's order books say what has really been bought, each purchase with its goods, who it's for and where the goods are now: `household_orders`, the household's purchases, when the worker is of the household, and `shop_orders`, the sales and sends of the shop the worker keeps; `orders`, when present, lists other deliveries still to come that the worker is party to, as the shop's keeper or the buyer (with who takes them up, `sent_up_by`). If the activity makes up, packs, carries, delivers or looks for goods shown there as still to be delivered, say in the summary that the order is in hand, and that the courier takes it up by the time due with nothing more for anyone to do. Never write that a parcel or basket of them is left here, or that the goods weren't there. Writing, reading or going over a list or an order buys nothing. When the scene has an order book and the activity is about an order it doesn't show, nothing has been bought or sent for it: never say it's in hand, ordered or on its way, and say in the summary that it isn't in the order book yet.
