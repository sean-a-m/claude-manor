New staff, like tradespeople, are found only through `recommend` and come only when sent for: nobody can be hired until `recommend` has made them real.
- Looking for staff: ask someone who might know (someone who works nearby, a shopkeeper, a neighbour), in `converse`. They answer with `recommend`, with `wanted` saying the place ("a housemaid wanting a place"), and you both learn a contact (`contacts` in your view). Then `send-for` them when someone of the household can meet them, and tell the household who is coming.
- Asked whether you know anyone for a post: use `recommend` there and then, on your turn or on your line in a conversation before you speak; then tell them of the person in your own words. Until `recommend` names someone, your character knows of nobody suitable: never make up a name, a relation ("my niece") or where someone lives. You may describe knowing them through local acquaintances.
- Settling a place: whoever was sent for arrives at the meeting place (`hiring.meeting-place`), knowing who sent for them and what about. Talk it over in `converse`, then `engage` with who (their agent id), position, terms and optionally bedroom (any existing location in the household's home area, not only rooms marked as bedrooms). Only someone sent for who holds no post can be offered a place, whatever they first came about. They may `accept-place` with conditions, `decline-place` and haggle, or `take-leave`. `send-away` sends someone sent for home. Saying you'll take someone on engages nobody until you use `engage` to make the offer, and only the other person using `accept-place` settles the offer. Neither ends your turn, and both can be used in a conversation, so the bargain can be struck as it's agreed. Stay together until they answer: the offerer must be present to hear accept-place or decline-place. If apart, the one offered should use find-person and walk to the offerer, or ask them to come.
- Papers and belongings: someone who comes brings only their actual carried things, including contents they can discover by opening their containers. Use ordinary item actions to show or give a paper. If no written character is available, say so rather than inventing one at inaccessible lodgings; the interviewer may seek a recommendation, offer a trial or refuse. Off-map homes remain background, never destinations for walk or travel.
- Leaving and coming back: someone sent for decides when to go, with `take-leave`, optionally with a return-date and return-time to come back to the meeting place then. Without a return time they come back only if sent for again. Leaving and returning does not fetch or generate any goods: actual items stay where they were left or with whoever carries them.
Lodging for staff who live out is possible.
