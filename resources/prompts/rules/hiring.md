New staff are found only through `recommend`: nobody can be hired until it has made them real.
- Looking for staff: ask someone who might know of a candidate (someone who works nearby, a shopkeeper, a neighbour), in `converse`. They answer with `recommend`, and you both learn the candidate's name, id and what they know of them.
- Asked whether you know anyone for a post: use `recommend` there and then, on your turn or on your line in a conversation before you speak, with the position id and the asker's id in `for`; then tell them of the candidate in your own words. Until `recommend` names someone, your character knows of nobody suitable: never make up a name, a relation ("my niece") or where a candidate lives. Do not recruit or recommend established characters already living or working in the area; a new candidate who lives locally but is not yet present as a character is fine. You may describe knowing the candidate through local acquaintances.
- Asked to look out for someone on another's behalf: ask around in `converse` and have whoever knows someone `recommend` them to you. Then `send-for` them when someone of the household can meet them, and tell the household who is coming.
- Bringing a candidate: anyone who knows a candidate's id can `send-for` them; they arrive at the meeting place (`hiring.meeting-place`) next round, and that is where to meet them. {{arrivals}} A letter or message of your own brings no candidate.
- Interview and engage: interview by `converse`, then `engage` with who (their agent id), position, terms and optionally bedroom (any existing location in the household's home area, not only rooms marked as bedrooms). The candidate may `accept-place` with conditions, `decline-place` and haggle, or `take-leave`. `send-away` ends an unengaged visit. Saying you'll take someone on engages nobody until you use `engage` to make the offer, and only the other person using `accept-place` settles the offer. Neither ends your turn, and both can be used in a conversation, so the bargain can be struck as it's agreed. Stay together until the candidate answers: the offerer must be present to hear accept-place or decline-place. If apart, the candidate should use find-person and walk to the offerer, or ask them to come; once together, accept-place settles the offer.
- Waiting: an unengaged candidate remains available for a full 24 hours after arrival or their latest conversation (24 rounds without a game clock), unless explicitly sent away or they choose to abandon the application with `take-leave`. They may wait anywhere in the mapped area: agree where to find them, and do not send them out of the mapped area merely to await a decision. A candidate-home is off-map background, not a place to walk to; while their status is here, their actual map location is where to meet them.
- Leaving and coming back: `take-leave` takes a candidate out of the mapped area, with no turns until they come back. A candidate who means to come back (to fetch their things, say) gives `take-leave` a return-date and return-time, and arrives at the meeting place then; those who know them see it in their calendar. Without a return time they come back only if sent for. `send-for` brings an away candidate back sooner.
Lodging for staff who live out is possible.
