### Finding people and sending for them
Staff, tradespeople, craftsmen and merchants who aren't already here are found only through `recommend` and come only when sent for.
- Finding someone: ask someone who might know (a shopkeeper, a neighbour, anyone who works nearby) in `converse`. They answer with `recommend`, giving who or what is wanted in their own words in `wanted` ("a housemaid wanting a place", "a joiner to mend the stair tread") and the asker's id in `for`, and you both learn a contact (`contacts` in your view): someone who works on the map, or someone from elsewhere. Asked whether you know anyone, use `recommend` there and then, on your turn or on your line before you speak, and then tell them of the person in your own words. Until `recommend` names someone, your character knows of nobody for it: never make up a name, a relation ("my niece") or where someone lives. Someone already in the world may be recommended for a job, but can't be taken on for a place.
- Sending for them: anyone who knows a contact can `send-for` them with its id, and `about` if it's not what they were recommended for. They are expected at the meeting place (`hiring.meeting-place`) {{lead-hours}} hours later and appear in your calendar; tell the household who is coming. `send-for` doesn't end your turn and can be used on your line in a conversation. Someone who works nearby is told at once and comes if they're willing; if they won't, ask for someone else. A letter or message of your own brings nobody.
- Settling it when they come: they arrive knowing who sent for them and what about. Talk it over and settle what they're here for, whatever was first asked: a place with `engage` (`who` is their agent id; `position`, `terms`, and optionally `bedroom`, any existing location in the household's home area; staff who live out may lodge nearby), a work planned with `plan-work` given with `order-work` and paid at the price agreed, or a small job they simply do with `spend-time`, paid if at all with `pay`, as agreed. Either side may refuse. Only someone sent for who holds no post can be offered a place. Saying you'll take someone on engages nobody: `engage` makes the offer, and only their `accept-place` (with conditions, if any) or `decline-place` settles it. Neither ends the turn and both can be used in a conversation, but the offerer must be present to hear the answer: stay together, or if apart, the one offered uses `find-person` and walks to the offerer. `send-away` sends someone sent for home.
- Papers and belongings: someone who comes brings only the things they actually carry, including what's in their containers: a servant seeking a place may have a written character, a tradesperson the tools of the trade. Show or give a paper with ordinary item actions. If there's none, say so rather than inventing one left at home; the one asking may seek another recommendation, offer a trial or refuse. Homes off the map are background, never places to walk or travel to.
- Leaving: someone sent for decides when to go, with `take-leave`, optionally with a return-date and return-time to come back to the meeting place then; otherwise they come back only if sent for again. Nothing sends them home by itself; someone staying the night sleeps where the sleep rules above say. Leaving and coming back fetches no goods: things stay where they were left or with whoever carries them. Taking a place ends the visit.
