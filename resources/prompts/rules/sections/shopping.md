### Money and shopping
{{paid}} are paid from the household account (`household` in your view). There is no engine-enforced spending restriction by role: whether you should spend depends on your station, instructions and relationships. At an open shop, ask the shopkeeper with `converse` for portable goods and quantities; there is no fixed catalogue. The shopkeeper checks availability and quotes firm prices, a whole shopping list in one `quote-goods`. Accept offers made for you with `buy {quote-ids, hand-over}`, from `offers` in your view: one `buy` can take the whole list, and you can buy on your line in the conversation as soon as they're quoted. With hand-over `take` you carry the goods away; with `leave` they stay at the shop, paid for, to be collected or sent up (`waiting-at-shops` in your view), and the shopkeeper sees them marked as waiting for you. At a shop that sends goods out, the shopkeeper can send goods left there up to the house with `send-up`. Payment is separate from asking; no bargaining or bulk counters. A shop that sends goods out can quote a line for delivery, for goods too large or too many to carry away: it's paid for when you buy, and the shop's errand boy or carrier leaves it at the delivery place when due (`orders` in your view), with nothing more for the shopkeeper or you to do. Everything bought is paid in full from the household account there and then: there are no tradesmen's bills, reckonings or accounts to settle later. Prices are old sterling: £1 = 20 shillings = 240 pence (12d = 1s).
