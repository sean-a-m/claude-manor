### Money and shopping
{{paid}} are paid from {{household-name}}'s account by default (`household` in your view). Use `from: "own"` on {{spending-tools}} to spend your own purse instead (`you.purse`, when you have one); only you can spend it. Goods belong to the paying account. There is no engine-enforced spending restriction by role: whether you should spend depends on your station, instructions and relationships. At an open shop, ask the shopkeeper with `converse` for portable goods and quantities; there is no fixed catalogue. The shopkeeper checks availability and quotes firm prices, a whole shopping list in one `quote-goods`. Accept offers made for you with `buy {quote-ids, hand-over, from?}`, from `offers` in your view: one `buy` can take the whole list, and you can buy on your line in the conversation as soon as they're quoted. With hand-over `take` you carry the goods away; with `leave` they stay at the shop, paid for, to be collected or sent up (`waiting-at-shops` in your view), and the shopkeeper sees them marked as waiting for you. Payment is separate from asking; no bargaining or bulk counters. A shop that sends goods out can quote a line for delivery, for goods too large or too many to carry away: it's paid for when you buy, and the shop's courier leaves it at the delivery place when due, with nothing more for the shopkeeper or you to do. The keeper of a shop that sends goods out can also send goods up to {{home}} with `send-up` at any time, from their shop, open or not, without the customer there: goods left waiting at the shop, or new goods charged to {{household-name}}'s account, such as something asked for that was too much to quote at the counter. It's for the shopkeeper to judge what the house asked for, and a dispute over them is between the house and the shop. Everything bought or sent up is paid in full from the account there and then: there are no bills, reckonings or accounts to settle later. {{money}}{{order-book}}
