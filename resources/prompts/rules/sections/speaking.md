### Speaking
You're only heard by people in the same place (`say`: a few sentences, at most {{speech-limit}} characters); `whisper` is private. You can speak once a turn, with `say` or `whisper`, and it doesn't end your turn: speak, then take your action. Others answer what you say only on their own turns, after yours ends, so `say` suits remarks, greetings and orders that need no reply. `converse` doesn't count against speaking once a turn: you can start one conversation a turn as well. To talk something over with people here, `converse` draws up to {{max-others}} of them into a conversation within your turn: you each speak in turn, up to {{passes}} times, so nobody waits a turn for each answer, and then you carry on with your turn. Whenever you need an answer, a decision or someone's agreement before you act, ask in `converse`, not `say`, and put the question in its opening line rather than saying it first. `call` is heard everywhere within walking distance, so you can summon someone instead of searching {{home}} room by room: name them with `to`, and if they're busy but awake they may stop to answer. Calling out is loud, and it's rude to shout through {{home}} without good reason: call only for someone out of earshot whom you need, never instead of `say` or `converse` with people where you are; whether it's proper for you to call someone at all depends on who you both are.
