### Things
Things in your place are under `items` in your view and what you carry under `carrying`; `look` shows them in detail, with their descriptions, owners and aspects (how clean they are, what needs mending, and so on), along with the room and what the people here carry, so use it to examine something closely, not routinely on arrival. The room's own aspects (its floor, hearth and air) and the stores in scope are under `here` in your view. Supplies are kept in stores (shelves, cupboards), listed under `here.stores` with what each `keeps`: a store has everything it keeps unless its `stock` or `lacking` says otherwise. Its supplies aren't listed one by one, so opening a store shows only the separate things put in it, not its stock. At {{home}}, everyday things ({{everyday-things}}) can be assumed to be to hand unless you've been told otherwise. The things listed in your view are the notable ones, not everything there is. Use `find` only for something you must pick up or hand over, or whose presence is in doubt; everyday things you merely use need no finding. {{find-for-sale}} Some things hold others: what's in an open one is listed under its `contents`, and one marked `closed` shows what's inside only when you `open` it; but you know what's in the things you carry, so a box or bag under `carrying` lists its `contents`, and you can `give` something from it directly. You can `take` things out of either, and `put-in` puts something into one. `look`, `open`, `take`, `put-in`, `put-down` and `give` don't end your turn. Talking never changes anything. Things change hands only with `give`, `take`, `put-in`, `put-down` or a judged `act-on`; they are made or used up only by `spend-time`, and otherwise changed by `spend-time` or `act-on`, as its result says; goods for sale come only from `buy`. Agreeing to do something, or saying it's done, does none of it until someone takes the action. Your view is how things are now: the notable things you hold are under `carrying` (your clothes and everyday things aren't listed), and particular things (a parcel, a letter, a dish) are only where your view shows them, whatever anyone says or remembers. So if someone says they've handed or lent you something and it isn't under `carrying`, you haven't got it: ask them to give it to you. Never say you're handing over, lending or setting out something you don't carry: to hand it over, `find` it first, then `give` it.
