belongings: REQUIRED only because belongings-mode is first-arrival. Return an array, including [] when nothing is appropriate. Write the background and probable possessions together, consistently with the supplied arrival-context: they have been sent for, and its line says what they are and do, its wanted what they were asked about. From those, decide what they'd bring on this occasion. Someone seeking a place in service may bring a box of their things and a written character from a former employer; a tradesperson come to do a job brings the tools of the trade they'd carry, perhaps a card or a trade letter; others bring little. Supply a few ordinary portable things brought on this occasion, not everything owned or an inventory of clothes being worn. Modest ordinary lives and few belongings are preferable to elaborate kit.

{{setting-guidance}}

Each object has name (at most {{max-name}} characters) and desc (at most {{max-desc}}). Optional fields: container ("open" or "closed"), aspects (a map of at most {{max-item-aspects}} lower-case word/hyphenated keys, each at most 24 characters, to nonblank phrases of at most {{max-aspect}} characters), consumable (boolean), contents (array of separate ordinary objects), reference? (boolean). At most {{max-roots}} top-level objects and {{max-objects}} objects total. Contents require a container. Only one level of contents; child objects cannot have container or contents.

reference? true marks at most one object: a paper that speaks to this person's character or credentials (a written character from a former employer, a master's letter for a tradesman), which the engine then ties to this person. It is not a special kind of item. Use it only when such a paper fits who they are and why they came; most people sent for about a job carry none.

Summarise ordinary contents as a contents aspect, rather than enumerate garments, combs and mending tools as objects. A letter may be summarised there when it needs no separate handling. When likely to be shown or handed over, supply a separate ordinary letter item inside the container; its desc holds its words or substance. The contents aspect describes the remaining things, never a second copy of that letter, and never private letter text. Keep invented past employment consistent across background and paper; a former employer's signature creates neither an agent nor a map location.

The tools a tradesperson works with are their own ordinary portable things, like anyone's belongings: a few hand tools, perhaps in a bag or box. Stock is different: goods, materials or parts for sale or to be supplied and charged for. Do not supply stock; whatever a job needs beyond the tools they carry is bought or ordered in play.

Possessions belong here, not solely in circumstances or appearance. Prose may refer to a supplied thing but cannot grant extra items or an off-map cache. Do not supply money or account balances, authority-granting deeds, business stock, purchases, delivery orders, other people's possessions, capabilities or supernatural effects. A document's words are not executable authority. Do not supply ids, holders, owners, places, waiting/order markers, arbitrary state paths or effects: the engine owns those. These descriptions remain private until actual arrival and never refill inventory on return.
